Summary
The Federal Reserve Board (Board) provides students opportunities for career success. The Board’s Internship program is designed to provide valuable work experience for undergraduate and graduate students considering careers including, but not limited to, economics, finance and accounting, information systems, and data analysis. The Board believes that by combining academic endeavors with project-related work experience, qualified and capable college students can gain the practical knowledge need in a competitive global job market. Internships are paid positions with salaries commensurate with credit hours; internships may be in-person, remote or hybrid, depending on the needs of the team. Employment in the Board’s internship program is granted to U.S. citizens. Applicants must be currently enrolled in an undergraduate or graduate degree program at an accredited university and returning to continue studies after the internship. Interns receive a syllabus or research project plan outlining their projects and goals for the internship. Interns are assigned a supervisor or mentor to guide them through their internship, and regular check-in meetings with their manager to manage performance expectations and assist with learning goals. Interns have access to a wide range of training programs and events for professional development purposes.
The intern will be joining the Office of the Chief SI Officer (“OCSIO”) supporting the SI Governance & PMO unit. The OCSIO sits within the Information Technology Division and is responsible for coordinating the Board’s use of super intelligence (SI/AI), breaking down barriers to innovation, cultivating staff skills for effective SI use, and managing the risks of SI use. Within the OCSIO, the SI Governance & PMO unit oversees SI enablement, governance, and program operations for the Board, working within the overall strategy and direction set by the Chief SI Officer. The team develops and matures the Board’s SI governance framework and usage policies, manages day-to-day program operations such as access to SI tools, tracks SI adoption, business value, risk posture, and program costs, and leads change management to support responsible adoption of SI across the Board. The team works closely with senior leaders across the Board and the Federal Reserve System and with business units such as Board Legal, Enterprise Risk Management, and Public Affairs, and partners with the OCSIO’s data science and engineering teams.
Expected Projects: The intern will support a variety of projects spanning governance, program operations, and metrics and reporting. Sample projects could include:
Developing SI adoption and usage dashboards and briefing materials for Board leadership
Analyzing SI tool usage and cloud consumption data to inform cost management
Researching SI governance practices and government SI directives to support policy development and compliance reporting
Tracking emerging developments in SI technology and governance across the financial and regulatory landscape
Supporting cross-agency collaboration and discussions with external organizations on emerging SI topics
Supporting SI use case review and consultation
Designing training and change management materials and guidance to help staff adopt approved SI tools
Creating and updating internal SI webpages and applications
Designing promotional materials and coordinating logistics for SI events
Suggested Major/Minor: Computer Science, Data Science, Artificial Intelligence, Political Science or Government, Public Policy, Economics, or a related field of study
Other majors will be considered provided candidates have demonstrable analytical and communication skills.
Required Skills and Knowledge:
Strong communication skills – Ability to write clearly and concisely for a non-technical audience. Comfortable preparing materials for and communicating with an executive audience.
Strong analytical skills – Ability to gather, organize, and analyze data (e.g., usage, cost, or survey data) and present findings in a clear, actionable format.
Strong organizational skills and attention to detail – Ability to manage competing priorities, track multiple initiatives and commitments, and ensure consistent follow-through.
Foundational knowledge of AI concepts – Understand at a high level how generative AI tools work, their common uses, and the risks they introduce (e.g., data privacy, security, model performance).
Collaboration and stakeholder awareness – Ability to work with technical and non-technical colleagues across organizational levels and handle sensitive information with discretion.
Preferred Skills: Experience with data analysis and visualization tools (e.g., Excel, Power BI, Python, or R); Familiarity with AI governance frameworks or government AI directives; Experience with generative AI tools (e.g., Claude, Copilot, ChatGPT) and AI coding tools; Experience developing training, communications, or change management materials; Experience working in Agile environments.
