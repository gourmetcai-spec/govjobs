## Duties

Provides senior technical leadership for enterprise ICAM programs, including identity governance, privileged access management, authentication, federation, and Zero Trust initiatives.
Collaborates across organizations to develop strategy, modernize identity services, implement secure access solutions, and advance cybersecurity capabilities supporting mission and enterprise objectives.

## Qualifications

Qualifications required:
10+ years of cybersecurity, IAM, or ICAM experience.
Experience leading enterprise identity modernization initiatives.
Experience operating in federal environments.
Experience serving as a technical lead, architect, or senior engineer.
Demonstrated ownership of strategy, roadmap development, and execution.
Experience supporting federal cybersecurity, Zero Trust, and compliance requirements.
Core ICAM Technical Expertise Required in Areas of: Identity Governance & Administration (IGA); Privileged Access Management (PAM); Access Management and Federation; Authentication and MFA; PKI and Certificate Management; Credential Lifecycle Management; Role-Based and Attribute-Based Access Control; Zero Trust Architecture.
Technologies which must have strong hands on experience: Microsoft Entra ID; Okta; SailPoint / IGA; CyberArk / PAM; PKI and certificate services; Federation (SAML, OIDC, OAuth); Zero Trust identity architectures; Lightweight Directory Access Protocol; Cloud identity services (Azure, AWS)
Certifications Required (at least one of the following): Certified Identity and Access Management (CIAM); Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP); CompTIA Security +
Competencies: Define technical roadmaps; Lead cross-functional teams without formal supervisory authority; Oversee modernization efforts; Mentor engineers and administrators; Communicate effectively with senior leadership and executive stakeholders; Drive automation and process improvement; Translate mission requirements into technical solutions

 Department of Homeland Security Headquarters

 DHS is calling on those who want to help protect American interests and secure our Nation. DHS Components work collectively to prevent terrorism; secure borders and our transportation systems; protect the President and other dignitaries; enforce and administer immigration laws; safeguard cyberspace; and ensure resilience to disasters.

The DHS Joint Duty Program (JDP) is an intra- and inter-departmental program which offers civilian personnel professional and developmental opportunities. Joint Duty Assignments (JDAs) enhance operations and mission execution through unity of effort and collaboration.

The DHS JDP is a human capital program initiative which is managed by OCHCO at DHS headquarters. The DHS JDP's purpose is two-fold: to encourage and to facilitate assignments of DHS personnel to other DHS components as well as outside agencies. The purpose of JDAs is to gain multi-component, multi-faceted experience focused on cross-cutting DHS and Federal-wide operations to enhance performance of the individual, the organizations, and the Department. JDAs are for a period not to exceed 1 year and do not qualify for Joint Duty credit.

The DHS JDP covers current Federal employees at the GS-12 through GS-15 grade levels and equivalent. The DHS JDP does not apply to members of the military service or contractors. JDAs do not offer Temporary Promotions.
